Know Your Rights

Every resident in a New York City shelter or transitional housing site has basic legal protections, regardless of immigration status or disability.

  • Right to Safety Every resident has an absolute right to a safe environment. Violence, intimidation, or harassment from staff, contractors, or other residents must be documented and addressed.
  • Right to Non-Retaliation It is unlawful for shelter operators to punish you for reporting problems, asking for accommodations, requesting medical care, or seeking legal or community support.
  • Right to Disability Accommodation Under the Americans with Disabilities Act (ADA), transitional shelters must provide reasonable accommodations including accessible living spaces and medically necessary support.
  • Right to Language Access Critical shelter information, rules, and legal notices should be available in a language you understand. Ask for interpretation and keep written records of each request.
  • Right to Document Complaints You have a right to make a complaint, ask that it be logged and dated, and receive a copy. Keep evidence organized by date for legal proceedings.
  • Right to Undocumented Residents New York City shelter access cannot be withheld or conditioned on immigration status. You retain legal protections. Seek trusted legal aid or advocacy support.

How to Document What Happened

Strong cases begin with clear timelines and consistent documentation. Here is what to preserve before speaking with a lawyer or official.

Evidence to Keep

  • Dates, times, and exact locations
  • Names of staff, residents, or witnesses
  • What happened, in your own words
  • Photos or videos (if safe to take)
  • Text messages, emails, or written notices
  • Medical records or treatment notes
  • Any notice of retaliation or punishment

Build Your Case

Organize records chronologically. Document patterns: repeated harassment, denial of accommodation, unsafe conditions, retaliation after complaints, or threats related to immigration status or disability.

Independent legal advocates and civil rights organizations use detailed evidence to pursue compensation and hold facilities accountable.

Official Accountability Email Template

Customize and send this email to city officials. It documents systemic failures, requests immediate investigation, and calls for protection of residents.

Copy and Customize
Subject: URGENT: Request for Investigation and Protection of Residents at [Facility Name]

To: NYC Department of Homeless Services Commissioner, Mayor's Office, City Council Members, Oversight Committees,

I am writing to formally request an immediate investigation and intervention regarding ongoing conditions at [Facility Name], which is operating as a [safe haven / transitional housing site / shelter] in New York City.

I am concerned about repeated reports of:
- physical violence, intimidation, or assault;
- retaliation against residents who report safety issues;
- unsafe conditions placing residents at serious risk;
- denial of reasonable accommodations for disability-related needs;
- insufficient language access and communication support;
- failure to log, document, or respond to complaints.

Residents at this location—including highly vulnerable individuals with disabilities, medical conditions, and undocumented status—deserve safety, dignity, and legal protection.

I am requesting that the City:
1. Conduct an immediate, unannounced inspection of the facility
2. Review all incident logs, complaints, staffing records, and oversight reports
3. Ensure language access and disability accommodation services are available
4. Guarantee no retaliation against residents who report conditions
5. Provide a formal timeline and written response to affected residents

City-funded shelters and safe havens are legally bound to preserve human dignity and guarantee physical safety. This issue requires urgent action.

Sincerely,
[Your Name]
[Your Phone / Email]
[Resident, family member, advocate, or legal representative]
