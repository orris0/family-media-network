Our Mission

Family Media Network centers survivors of assaults and abuse in New York City Safe Haven and transitional shelters—especially disabled and undocumented people—seeking safety, help, accountability, and pathways to support or compensation. We focus on facilities including The Kelly (Safe Haven), The Andrews (Breaking Ground), Travellers / Traveler’s Safe Haven, Breaking Ground programs, and BRC transitional / Safe Haven sites (including the 25th Street area).

This is a community advocacy resource. It is not affiliated with NYC agencies or shelter operators.

Context on the Shelters

Public reporting and city data have documented elevated violence, serious incidents, and systemic challenges across parts of the NYC shelter system, including Safe Havens and BRC-operated sites. Individual experiences vary. This site does not make or verify specific unproven allegations against named individuals.

Named focus areas include:

  • The Kelly / XX Street Kelly Safe Haven
  • The Andrews Safe Haven (Breaking Ground)
  • Traveler’s / Travellers Safe Haven
  • Breaking Ground programs
  • BRC transitional and Safe Haven programs (including historical 25th Street area concerns)

Know Your Rights

This is not legal advice. Always consult an independent advocate or attorney.

  • Right to shelter – In NYC, people experiencing homelessness generally have a right to shelter. Immigration status does not bar access to emergency shelter or drop-in services.
  • Safety & complaints – You have the right to a safe environment and to file complaints about assaults, abuse, harassment, or retaliation. Document incidents safely and report to shelter staff, DHS processes, and (for crimes) NYPD.
  • Disability accommodations – DHS must provide reasonable accommodations so people with disabilities can access shelter and services equally. Request them from staff (verbally or in writing). Appeal denials.
  • Gender identity protections – Placement consistent with gender identity and related accommodations are protected under city policy.
  • Language access – Right to interpreter services for key interactions.

Key contacts: Coalition for the Homeless • Legal Aid Society / Legal Services NYC • Sanctuary for Families • Disability Rights NY • NYC DV Hotline 800-621-HOPE (4673) • 911 for immediate danger.

Take Action

Copy and adapt the email template below. Send to your City Council member, DHS leadership, relevant committees, and advocacy groups. Keep copies.

Sample Email to City Officials

Subject: Urgent: Safety Concerns at [Shelter Name(s)] – Support for Disabled & Undocumented Survivors

Dear [Official / Council Member / DHS Commissioner],

I am writing regarding safety concerns and the need for accountability at New York City Safe Haven and transitional shelters, including [The Kelly, The Andrews / Breaking Ground, Traveler’s Safe Haven, BRC sites].

Residents—particularly those who are disabled or undocumented—face heightened vulnerability. Survivors need safety improvements, proper incident response, full implementation of reasonable accommodations, protection from retaliation, independent advocacy, and pathways to redress.

I urge your office to investigate conditions, meet with survivors (with privacy protections), strengthen oversight, and support policy and funding changes that prioritize dignity and safety.

Please respond with the steps you will take.

Sincerely,
[Your name or “Concerned Survivor/Advocate”]
[Optional contact]

For any fundraising or compensation campaigns, use transparent, legal platforms and clear goals. Do not promise outcomes.
