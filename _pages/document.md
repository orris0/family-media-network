Documentation can help an advocate understand what happened, but your safety comes first. Only collect or share information when it is safe and lawful where you are.

## A private incident log

Use your own words. Record the date and time, site or common area, people involved, what you directly experienced, injuries or damage, witnesses, reports made, reference numbers, and the response. Mark what you know firsthand versus what someone told you.

## Preserve, do not publish

Keep original messages, notices, photos, medical paperwork, and complaint responses in a secure place. Make a backup outside the shelter only if safe. Redact names, faces, room numbers, medical details, and immigration information before sharing. Ask an advocate how to transfer files securely.

## Ask for records

You can ask for copies of your own complaints and accommodation requests. Write down when you asked, who received the request, and the deadline they gave you. An advocate can explain whether a public-records request or other process fits your situation.

<p class="fmn-small"><strong>Do not confront a person who harmed you to gather proof.</strong> A trusted advocate can help you plan a safer next step.</p>
